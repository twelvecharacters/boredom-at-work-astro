Black Friday is in nine days. The sales emails are already piling up, and the easiest way to overspend is to walk into the week without a number. A budgeting app fixes that in an afternoon: you decide what the holiday budget is, the app shows you what is left, and the "deal" that is not in the plan stays in the cart.

I compared the seven budgeting apps worth paying attention to in 2026. Each has a different method, and the method matters more than the feature list. Here is how they line up.

## 1. YNAB, Best for Changing How You Spend

YNAB (You Need A Budget) is less an app than a method. Every dollar that arrives gets assigned to a category before you spend it. Rent, groceries, the Black Friday fund, all of it. When the category is empty, you move money from somewhere else, on purpose, or you do not buy the thing.

That friction is the point. It is also why people either love YNAB or quit within a month. If you want an app that quietly reports where money went, this is not it.

- **Price:** $14.99 per month or $109 per year, which works out to $9.08 a month. The trial is 34 days with no card required, and college students get a full year free with proof of enrollment.
- **Bank sync:** yes, with the usual aggregator hiccups when a bank changes its login.
- **Strength:** the method. Nothing else forces the "what is this money for" question as consistently.
- **Weakness:** the price, and the learning curve in week one.

**Black Friday use:** create a "Holiday gifts" category now, fund it from this month's paycheck, and shop only from that number.

## 2. Monarch Money, Best All-in-One Dashboard

Monarch is where many Mint users landed. You connect every account, including investments and loans, and get net worth, cash flow, recurring bills, and a category budget in one place. Shared access for a partner is built in, which is the feature that keeps couples on it.

- **Price:** $14.99 per month or $99.99 per year. The first week is free, and new users currently get 30 percent off the first year with the code WELCOME.
- **Bank sync:** yes, and it is the core of the product.
- **Strength:** the complete picture, plus collaboration.
- **Weakness:** no free tier, and the budgeting is looser than YNAB's. You can overspend a category and the app will only tell you afterward.

## 3. Copilot Money, Best Design for Apple Users

Copilot is the prettiest app in this list and the only one that feels native on iPhone and Mac. Transaction categorization learns from your corrections, the charts are genuinely useful, and the widgets make checking the budget a two-second glance.

- **Price:** $95 per year, about $7.92 a month, after a one-month free trial.
- **Platforms:** iPhone, iPad, Mac, and web. There is no Android app, which rules it out for mixed households.
- **Strength:** design and automatic categorization.
- **Weakness:** Apple only, and the budgeting is category-based rather than zero-based.

## 4. EveryDollar, Best Free Zero-Based Budget

EveryDollar comes from Ramsey Solutions and uses the same zero-based idea as YNAB: income minus every planned expense equals zero. The free version does that with manual transaction entry, which some people prefer because typing in a purchase makes you feel it.

- **Price:** free, for as long as you want. Premium adds bank sync and reporting for $79.99 a year, about $6.67 a month, after a 14-day trial.
- **Strength:** a complete zero-based budget at no cost.
- **Weakness:** the free version has no bank connection, and the app leans into the Ramsey ecosystem.

**Black Friday use:** the manual entry is actually an advantage during a sale week. Every purchase you type in is a purchase you noticed.

## 5. Goodbudget, Best for Envelope Budgeting

Goodbudget is the digital version of the cash-envelope system. You fill envelopes at the start of the month and spend from them. It is deliberately simple and syncs across devices, so both partners see the same envelopes.

- **Price:** free with 10 regular envelopes, 10 more envelopes, one account, two devices, and one year of history. Premium is $10 a month or $80 a year for unlimited envelopes and accounts, five devices, and seven years of history.
- **Strength:** the envelope model, and the free plan is genuinely usable.
- **Weakness:** manual entry only, no bank sync at any tier.

## 6. Quicken Simplifi, Cheapest Full-Featured Subscription

Simplifi is Quicken's modern app, and right now it is the cheapest way to get bank sync, a spending plan, and reports in one subscription. The "spending plan" shows what is safe to spend after bills and savings, which is exactly the number you want during a sale.

- **Price:** $3.99 per month billed annually for new memberships through November 6, 2026, then the regular $6.99 per month. Renewal is at the then-current rate, so note the date.
- **Strength:** price and a clean spending-plan view.
- **Weakness:** the intro price expires, and the app is less customizable than Monarch.

## 7. Rocket Money, Best for Killing Subscriptions

Rocket Money (formerly Truebill) is the app to try if the problem is not the budget but the twelve subscriptions draining it. It finds recurring charges, shows them in one list, and the Premium tier will cancel them for you and negotiate some bills.

- **Price:** the free tier tracks spending and lists subscriptions. Premium is priced on a sliding scale you pick inside the app, so the exact amount depends on what you choose.
- **Strength:** subscription discovery. Most people find at least one charge they forgot.
- **Weakness:** the budgeting features are thinner than the dedicated apps above, and the price is not published on the website.

## Which Budgeting App Should You Choose?

- **You want to change your habits:** YNAB. Accept the learning curve.
- **You want the whole financial picture, shared with a partner:** Monarch.
- **You are all-in on Apple and want it to look good:** Copilot.
- **You want zero-based budgeting for free:** EveryDollar.
- **You think in envelopes:** Goodbudget.
- **You want the cheapest subscription with bank sync:** Simplifi, before the intro price ends.
- **Your subscriptions are out of control:** Rocket Money first, then one of the others.

1. **Pick one app and start the trial today.** Every app above has a free tier or trial, and the sale is next week.
2. **Create one category called "Holiday."** Fund it with a real number from this month's income, not a wish.

4. **Log purchases the same day.** On Black Friday itself, check the category before each checkout. When it reads zero, you are done.
