> **Disclaimer:** This article explains how cashback and deal apps work. Nothing here is financial advice. Rates and bonuses change daily, especially during sale weeks. Details were checked on each service's own website on October 9, 2026.

Black Friday is in a week. The sale prices are mostly set, so the only lever you still control is what you get back on top: cashback, coupons, and knowing whether the discount is real. The six apps below do those three jobs. None of them cost anything, all of them are paid by the store, and together they take about ten minutes to set up.

None of the links in this article are affiliate links.

## Quick Comparison

| App | Job | How you earn | Payout |
|-----|-----|--------------|--------|
| **Rakuten** | Cashback portal | Click through before shopping | Check or PayPal, paid quarterly |
| **TopCashback** | Cashback portal | Click through before shopping | Bank, PayPal, Venmo, gift cards |
| **Honey (PayPal)** | Coupon extension | Codes tested at checkout, Gold Rewards | Gift cards |
| **Capital One Shopping** | Coupon extension + price watch | Codes at checkout, rewards on purchases | Rewards credits |
| **Ibotta** | Grocery and in-store cashback | Offers in the app, receipts | Cash from the app |
| **Keepa** | Amazon price history | No earning, it stops you overpaying | n/a |

## 1. Rakuten, The Cashback Portal With the Most Stores

Rakuten is the portal most people start with. You open Rakuten, click through to the store, and a percentage of what you spend comes back. The percentage varies by store and is raised for many of them during Black Friday week. Payouts arrive on a quarterly schedule by check or PayPal, which Rakuten calls the Big Fat Check.

- **Strength:** the store list. Almost every large US retailer is in it.
- **Weakness:** quarterly payouts, and the browser extension needs to be the only portal extension running or the last-click credit gets messy.
- **Black Friday tip:** Rakuten regularly runs a new-member bonus tied to a first purchase. If you have never signed up, check the current offer before your first order of the week.

## 2. TopCashback, Higher Base Rates

TopCashback works the same way and competes on rate. Its own pitch is that it passes 100 percent of the retailer's commission to the member, so for the same store the rate is often a little higher than Rakuten's. It charges no membership fees, and you can withdraw to a bank account, PayPal, Venmo, or gift cards, some of which carry a bonus of up to 5 percent.

- **Strength:** rates and flexible payout.
- **Weakness:** cashback can take weeks to move from pending to payable, and the interface is busier.
- **Black Friday tip:** before each purchase, check the rate for that store on both portals. Thirty seconds, and the difference on a $500 order is real money.

## 3. Honey (PayPal), Coupons Without the Hunt

Honey is the extension that pops up at checkout and tries coupon codes for you. It covers more than 30,000 sites, and it also runs Gold Rewards, points on eligible purchases that you redeem for gift cards. Its Droplist feature watches the price of items you save and tells you when they drop.

- **Strength:** zero effort. It just appears at checkout.
- **Weakness:** Gold Rewards is paid from the same affiliate commission as the portals, so Honey can take the last click away from Rakuten or TopCashback. Decide which one gets the credit.
- **Black Friday tip:** use Honey for the coupon test, but if you clicked through a portal, decline Honey's rewards activation on that order so the portal keeps the credit.

## 4. Capital One Shopping, Coupons Plus Price Drops

Capital One Shopping does what Honey does, with one addition: it watches prices on products you viewed and purchased and tells you when they drop. You do not need a Capital One card. It applies coupon codes automatically, compares prices across sellers, and shares part of its commission as rewards.

- **Strength:** price-drop alerts on things you already bought, which matters when a store cuts the price again on Cyber Monday.
- **Weakness:** same last-click conflict as Honey. Run one coupon extension, not both.

## 5. Ibotta, The Grocery Run

Ibotta is a different category. It pays cashback on groceries and in-store purchases: you activate offers in the app, buy the item, and submit the receipt or link a retailer account. During Black Friday it also runs online offers, but the grocery side is where it earns its place.

- **Strength:** the one app that covers Thanksgiving dinner.
- **Weakness:** offers are item-specific, so it rewards planning more than browsing.

## 6. Keepa, The Honesty Check

Keepa does not pay you anything. It charts the price history of any Amazon product, so you can see in one glance whether the Black Friday price is the lowest of the year or just the October price with a sale badge. The free tier is enough; CamelCamelCamel is a similar free alternative.

- **Strength:** it kills fake discounts.
- **Weakness:** Amazon only. For other stores, the Capital One Shopping and Honey price trackers are the fallback.

## How to Stack Them on Black Friday

1. **Check the price history first** (Keepa). If the discount is not real, stop here.
2. **Compare the cashback rate** for the store on Rakuten and TopCashback. Click through the winner.
3. **Let one coupon extension test codes** at checkout. If it finds one, great. If it asks to activate its own rewards, decline so the portal keeps the credit.
4. **Pay with the card that earns the most** on that category. The card reward is independent of everything above.
5. **Keep the receipts.** Price-drop alerts from Capital One Shopping are useless without proof of what you paid.

## Three Traps to Avoid

- **Chasing cashback into purchases you did not plan.** Five percent back on a $300 item you did not need is $285 gone, not $15 earned. Set the budget first; our [best budgeting apps](/best-budgeting-apps/) guide covers that in twenty minutes.
- **Two portal extensions at once.** They overwrite each other's tracking. One portal, one coupon extension.
- **Pending cashback as money.** It can take 30 to 90 days to become payable and gets voided on returns. Do not count it until it lands.
