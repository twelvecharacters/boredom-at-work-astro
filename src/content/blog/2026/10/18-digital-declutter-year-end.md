Your phone and laptop accumulate clutter the same way a junk drawer does: quietly, a little every day, until opening them feels heavier than it should. The end of the year is the perfect time to reset. This declutter takes an afternoon and leaves you with faster, calmer devices going into the new year.

(In the mood to reduce the physical pile too? Our guide on [how to stop buying tech you do not need](/stop-buying-tech-you-dont-need/) pairs well with this.)

## 1. Clear the Desktop and Downloads

Start with the two folders that collect the most junk:

- **Desktop:** file the handful of things you need, delete the rest, and aim for a clean background. A tidy desktop genuinely feels better to open.
- **Downloads:** this folder is almost always a graveyard of installers, PDFs, and screenshots. Sort what matters, delete the rest.

## 2. Back Up and Cull Your Photos

Photos are the biggest space hog on most devices:

- Make sure everything is **backed up** (cloud or an external drive) before you delete anything.
- Delete **duplicates, blurry shots, and screenshots** you no longer need.
- Create a few simple albums for the keepers so they are actually findable.

## 3. Uninstall Apps You Do Not Use

Scroll through your phone and laptop and be honest: if you have not opened an app in a few months, it is probably safe to remove. Fewer apps means fewer notifications, less background drain, and a home screen you can actually navigate.

## 4. Tame Your Inbox and Notifications

- **Unsubscribe** from newsletters you never read (most emails have a one-click link at the bottom).
- **Turn off notifications** for apps that do not need to interrupt you. Keep messages and calendar, silence the rest.
- Archive or file the inbox backlog so you start fresh.

## 5. Cancel Forgotten Subscriptions

This is the step that often pays for itself. Check your bank or app store for **recurring charges** and cancel anything you no longer use: a streaming service you forgot, a trial that became a subscription, an app you tried once. Most people find at least one.

## 6. Tidy the Physical Side

While you are at it, spend ten minutes on the desk itself: coil the cables, wipe the screens, and clear the clutter. Our [cable management tips](/cable-management-tips/) make the wires disappear, and a clean screen makes everything feel new. If you want tools for the software side, our [best free macOS software](/best-free-macos-software/) list has a few tidy-up helpers.

## Keep It From Piling Back Up

A big declutter only lasts if you add a tiny habit:

- **Weekly:** empty the downloads folder and close the tab hoard (five minutes).
- **Monthly:** delete apps you stopped using and glance at subscriptions.
- **Year-end:** the full reset above.

## The Bottom Line

A year-end digital declutter is one afternoon of work for a full year of lighter, faster devices. **Clear your desktop and downloads, back up and cull your photos, drop unused apps, quiet your notifications, and cancel forgotten subscriptions.** Then protect it with a five-minute weekly habit, and January will feel a lot calmer.
