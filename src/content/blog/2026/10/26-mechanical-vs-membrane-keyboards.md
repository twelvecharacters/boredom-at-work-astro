## 1. How They Work: The Engineering Difference

The fundamental difference between these two technologies comes down to **how a keystroke registers electrical contact**.

### Membrane Keyboards (Rubber Domes)
A standard membrane keyboard consists of three flexible plastic sheets printed with electrical circuit traces, separated by a rubber dome mat.

When you press a key:
1.  You push down against a silicone rubber dome.
2.  The dome resists, then abruptly collapses in a mushy "pop."
3.  You must press the key all the way down until the top conductive trace forcefully impacts the bottom trace against the rigid plastic backplate.

Because the rubber degrades, hardens, and oxidizes over time, membrane keyboards become increasingly stiff and uneven as they age.

### Mechanical Keyboards (Independent Switches)
A mechanical keyboard houses an independent physical switch mechanism beneath every single keycap. Inside each switch sits a plastic stem, a precision steel spring, and two copper contact leaves.

When you press a mechanical key:
1.  The stem glides smoothly down the housing channel.
2.  At roughly halfway through the keystroke (the **actuation point**), the stem separates or connects the metal leaves, instantly sending the keystroke signal to your computer.
3.  **You do not need to bottom out.** The keystroke registers in mid-air.

## 2. Typing Speed (WPM): Can a Keyboard Make You Faster?

Empirical typing tests consistently reveal that experienced touch-typists gain **5% to 15% higher Words Per Minute (WPM)** and fewer typographical errors when transitioning from a mushy membrane board to a well-tuned mechanical keyboard.

> **Actuation Mechanics and Typing Effort:**
> *   **Membrane Keystroke:** Finger presses down against rubber resistance, collapses the dome, and forcefully slams into the hard plastic plate at 100% travel depth. (High finger impact shock, sluggish key reset, muscle fatigue).
> *   **Mechanical Keystroke:** Finger glides smoothly down a precision steel spring, registers the keypress at roughly 50% travel depth in mid-air, and instantly rebounds. (Zero bottom-out required, faster stroke-to-stroke hand transitions).

### Why Mechanical Switches Accelerate Speed:
1.  **Immediate Tactile Feedback:** Tactile switches (like Gateron Brown, Holy Panda, or Baby Kangaroo) provide a subtle physical "bump" the microsecond the key registers. Your fingers learn to float effortlessly across the keys, reversing direction without wasting time or energy bottoming out.
2.  **Rapid Spring Rebound:** The internal steel spring forcefully pushes the key back up into resting position instantly, resetting the switch faster than a sluggish silicone dome can pop back into shape.
3.  **N-Key Rollover (NKRO):** Most membrane keyboards suffer from "ghosting" (failing to register more than 3 or 4 simultaneous keypresses). Mechanical keyboards feature full NKRO anti-ghosting, ensuring every single keystroke is registered even during rapid 120+ WPM typing bursts.

## 3. Ergonomics and Joint Fatigue: Saving Your Wrists

Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI) and carpal tunnel syndrome are among the most common desk ailments. How do keyboard switches affect joint health?

### The "Bottoming-Out" Shock Wave
When typing on a membrane keyboard, you slam your fingertips against a solid plastic substrate thousands of times a day. That impact energy travels directly up the flexor tendons of your fingers into your wrist joints and forearm muscles, contributing to inflammation and tendonitis.

Mechanical switches act like **shock absorbers**. Because the internal steel spring progressively increases resistance, your fingers naturally decelerate before hitting the bottom plate.

### The Height Warning (Wrist Angle)
There is one major ergonomic caveat with mechanical keyboards: **front height**.

Standard mechanical keyboards sit 18mm to 22mm off the desk surface. If you bend your wrists upward (wrist extension) to reach high keycaps, you compress the median nerve inside the carpal tunnel:
*   **The Fix:** Always pair a high-profile mechanical keyboard with a soft wooden or memory foam wrist rest, or use our recommended desk setup tips to maintain a neutral, floating forearm position.
*   **The Low-Profile Alternative:** If you dislike wrist rests, choose a **low-profile mechanical keyboard** (such as the NuPhy Air75 V2 or Keychron K3 Max), which provides mechanical switch mechanics at the slim thickness of an Apple Magic Keyboard.

## 4. Understanding Switch Types: Linear vs. Tactile vs. Clicky

If you decide to go mechanical, you must choose a switch category that fits your work environment:

> **The 3 Core Mechanical Switch Types:**
> *   **Linear (Red / Yellow Switches):** Completely smooth from top to bottom with zero bump. Whispering quiet, effortless gliding, and beloved by programmers, gamers, and rapid touch-typists.
> *   **Tactile (Brown / Banana Switches):** Features a distinct tactile bump at the actuation point. Ideal for writers and office workers who want tactile confirmation without loud noise.
> *   **Clicky (Blue / Jade Switches):** Produces a sharp mechanical click sound on every keystroke. Extremely satisfying for home offices, but strictly forbidden in shared open-plan offices.

---

## 5. The Scissor-Switch Exception: The Middle Ground

Not all membrane keyboards are created equal. Laptop keyboards and premium slim office boards (like the **Logitech MX Keys S** and **Apple Magic Keyboard**) use **Scissor-Switch mechanisms**.

Scissor-switches place a tiny mechanical X-shaped plastic stabilizer underneath the rubber dome. This eliminates the wobbly, uneven feeling of cheap membrane boards while keeping total travel very short (1.5mm).

*   **When Scissor-Switches Win:** If you travel frequently, switch between coffeeshops, or prefer laptop-style zero-travel finger gliding, a premium scissor-switch keyboard is a fantastic, highly portable compromise.
