If you want jaw-dropping detail, miniatures, tabletop models, jewelry, or figurines, resin 3D printing beats filament every time. The good news for 2026 is that beginner resin printers are cheaper and far more forgiving than they used to be. The catch is that resin is messier and more hazardous than filament, so there is more to know before you buy. Here are the best resin 3D printers for beginners, and what you actually need to get started.

(Not sure whether resin or filament is right for you? Our [FDM vs resin printing](/fdm-vs-resin-printing/) explainer breaks down the tradeoffs.)

## The Quick Picks

- **Best for most beginners:** Elegoo Mars 5 Ultra (around $269 to $338).
- **Cheapest credible option:** Anycubic Photon Mono 4 (around $169).
- **Best if you want a bigger build:** Elegoo Saturn 4 Ultra (around $549 to $599).

## Anycubic Photon Mono 4 (~$169): The Budget Entry

The **Photon Mono 4** is the realistic price floor for a credible new resin printer in 2026. You get a sharp 10K-class screen, a 7-inch build area, simple first-time leveling, and Anycubic's mature slicer support. For someone who wants to try resin without a big outlay, this is the entry point.

**Best for:** curious beginners on a tight budget who want to test the waters.

## Elegoo Mars 5 Ultra (~$269 to $338): The Best Starting Point

The **Mars 5 Ultra** is the one we would put most beginners on. It adds the things that make learning resin far less frustrating: **auto-leveling**, a **tilt-release** mechanism that improves print reliability, and **failure detection**. It shares much of its DNA with the pricier Saturn in a smaller, more affordable body.

**Best for:** most first-time resin users who want a forgiving, reliable machine.

## Elegoo Saturn 4 Ultra (~$549 to $599): Step Up in Size and Resolution

If you already know you want bigger prints or the finest detail, the **Saturn 4 Ultra** offers a 16K-class screen, a larger build volume, smart features like heating and AI failure detection, and WiFi. It is more than most beginners need, but it is the pick if you want room to grow.

**Best for:** ambitious beginners or hobbyists who want the best detail and a larger plate.

## The Part Nobody Tells Beginners: Resin Is Messy and Toxic

This matters more than which printer you pick. Uncured resin is an irritant and should be treated with care:

- **Ventilation is a must.** Print in a well-ventilated space, ideally with a respirator rated for fumes.
- **Wear nitrile gloves** every time you handle resin or a fresh print.
- **You need a wash-and-cure step.** Prints come out coated in sticky resin and must be washed (usually in isopropyl alcohol) and then cured under UV light. A wash-and-cure station makes this easy, or you can DIY with a tub and a UV lamp.
- **Cleanup is real.** Budget for paper towels, IPA, and a dedicated, protected work surface.

None of this should scare you off, plenty of beginners handle it fine, but go in knowing resin asks for more care and setup than filament.

## What Else You Need to Budget For

- **Nitrile gloves** and a **respirator** or strong ventilation.
- **Isopropyl alcohol (IPA)** for washing prints.
- **A wash-and-cure station** (or a tub plus a UV lamp).
- **A safe, protected workspace** away from living and sleeping areas.
