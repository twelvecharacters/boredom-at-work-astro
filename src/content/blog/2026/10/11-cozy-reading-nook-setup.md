The best way to read more is to make reading feel like a treat. A dedicated reading nook, even a small one, gives you a spot that quietly says "sit down and open a book." You do not need a spare room or a big budget. This is how to set one up in 2026, in the order that matters: spot, seat, light, surface, then the soft stuff.

## 1. Pick the Right Spot

Look for a corner that is:

- **Quiet,** away from the TV and the kitchen.
- **Near natural light,** ideally beside a window for daytime reading.
- **Out of the way,** so it stays a reading spot and does not become a dumping ground for mail and laundry.

A bay window, a spare corner of the bedroom, or the end of a hallway all work. The key is that it feels a little separate from the busy parts of the home. If the only free corner is next to your desk, that is fine too, as long as the chair faces away from the screen. (Upgrading that desk at the same time? Our [desk upgrade guide](/desk-upgrade-guide/) covers the work side of the room.)

**Small-apartment version:** a window sill wide enough for a cushion, or a single armchair angled into a corner with a floor lamp behind it. The nook is defined by the lamp and the chair, not by square footage.

## 2. Choose a Comfortable Seat

Comfort is everything, because an uncomfortable chair ends reading sessions early. Options that work:

- **An armchair** with real back support and arms low enough to rest a book on your lap. A high back lets you lean your head.
- **A window seat** with a firm cushion, plus a bolster for your back.
- **A large floor cushion or bean bag** for a relaxed nook, best for shorter sessions and for kids.
- **A recliner or chaise** if you read for hours and fall asleep doing it.

Whatever you choose, test it for an hour. Your back should be supported, your neck should not be craned down at the book, and your feet should either reach the floor or rest on something. A footstool or ottoman is not decoration; it is what keeps you in the chair past page twenty.

## 3. Get the Lighting Right

Lighting makes or breaks a nook. Aim for **warm, glare-free light** that lands on the page without hitting your eyes:

- **A warm-toned floor or table lamp** beside the chair, roughly at shoulder height, slightly behind you. Warm white (2700K to 3000K) is easier on the eyes in the evening than cool white.
- **A clip-on book light** for paper books, especially if someone else in the room is sleeping. It is also a lovely small gift, see our [gifts for book lovers](/best-gifts-for-book-lovers/) list.
- **Skip the overhead light.** Ceiling fixtures throw shadows onto the page and glare off glossy covers.

## 4. Add a Surface for the Essentials

You need somewhere to set down a mug, your book, and your glasses. A **small side table**, a stool, a C-shaped table that slides under the chair, or even a stack of sturdy books does the job. The test: can you put down a full mug without looking? If you have to twist, the table is in the wrong place.

## 5. Layer in the Cozy Touches

This is what turns a chair in a corner into a nook you want to sink into:

- **A throw blanket** and one or two soft cushions.
- **A small plant** to bring life to the corner.
- **A candle** or a warm scent, if you like one.
- **A little shelf or basket** for your current stack of books, so the next read is always within reach.
- **A rug,** even a small one, which marks the nook as its own space on a hard floor.

Keep it to a few pieces. The point is comfort, not a set from a catalog.

## 6. Sort Your Books and Your Reading Tech

Keep your current reads within arm's reach so you are never hunting for something to start. A small shelf, a magazine rack, or a basket works well. Three to five books in the basket is the sweet spot: enough choice, not enough to feel like a to-do list.

## 7. Keep the Phone Out

The nook only works if it is the one place the phone does not go. Charge it in another room, or at least out of arm's reach. It sounds trivial, and it is the single change that doubles reading time for most people. ## Keep It Clutter-Free

The fastest way to ruin a reading nook is to let it collect laundry, mail, and random objects. A nook only stays inviting if it stays clear. Give it a ten-second tidy when you get up, and it will always be ready for the next session.

## The Bottom Line

A cozy reading nook comes down to four things: **a comfortable seat, warm glare-free light, a surface for your drink and book, and a few soft, personal touches.** Find a quiet corner, make it comfortable, leave the phone elsewhere, and keep the corner clear.
